# Description
Bordering the city of [[2-World/Hubs/Neverwinter\|Neverwinter]] and stretching far beyond its outer districts, the [[2-World/Hubs/Neverwinter\|Neverwinter]] Woods form a dense, ancient forest that has long resisted full exploration or control. Towering trees, thick undergrowth, and winding, half-forgotten paths define the landscape, creating a space that feels both expansive and claustrophobic. The forest is a constant presence at the edge of civilisation—close enough to be familiar, yet vast enough to remain deeply unknown.
Hidden somewhere within these woods lies [[2-World/Hubs/Neverwood Academy\|Neverwood Academy]], concealed behind a powerful magical barrier that obscures its exact location. To those without knowledge or invitation, the forest subtly misdirects, bending paths, masking landmarks, and leading travellers away from the academy’s true position. Even seasoned rangers and local hunters speak of routes that seem to loop or vanish entirely when they stray too close to where Neverwood is said to be.
The woods themselves are far from empty. They are home to a wide range of creatures, both mundane and magical, including bands of mischievous xvarts, territorial owlbears, and other roaming beasts that thrive in the forest’s layered ecosystem. Some areas are relatively safe and frequently travelled, particularly those closer to [[2-World/Hubs/Neverwinter\|Neverwinter]], while deeper regions grow increasingly unpredictable and dangerous.
Despite these risks, the Neverwinter Woods remain an important resource for both the city and the academy. Students occasionally venture into its outer reaches under supervision to study its ecology, gather materials, or test survival skills. Yet even these controlled excursions carry an undercurrent of uncertainty, for the woods are not entirely passive. Paths shift, sounds travel strangely, and there is a persistent sense that something within the forest is always watching—aware of those who pass through it, and perhaps aware of the hidden academy it protects.
